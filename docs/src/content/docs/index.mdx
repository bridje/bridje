Bridje is a statically typed Lisp that runs on GraalVM.
It takes its inspiration from Clojure, Kotlin, Haskell and Erlang — and its syntax, deliberately, from the C/Java family: brackets where you'd expect them.

```bridje
ns: hello.core

decl: {.name Str, .email Str}

tag: User{.name, .email}

def: greet(user)
  println(.name(user))

def: main(args)
  greet(User{.name "James", .email "james@example.com"})
```

Underneath, that's s-expressions all the way down — `def: greet(user) ...` is `(def (greet user) ...)` — but you'll rarely need to write them that way.

:::caution[Pre-alpha]
Bridje is nowhere near finished.
What's documented here is what's built, and it will change — often.
:::

## Why Bridje?

I've written Clojure since 2012, and (subjectively) no other language comes close for the joy of programming: the REPL's feedback loop, a simple expression-oriented Lisp where every sub-expression can be picked up and moved, and immutable data that doesn't change underneath you.
It's a shame, then, that it's not nearly as mainstream as it should be — and I think the parentheses have a lot to answer for.

Bridje is an attempt to keep what makes a Lisp a joy to use, and to help with the parts of software that still need significant thought, however much experience we have:

- **A Lisp that doesn't look like one.** Three purely syntactic sugars — `foo(a, b)` calls, `Foo{…}` construction and indentation-based `foo:` blocks — mean Bridje reads like a mainstream language, while the code-as-data core stays intact.
- **Types that fit your domain, not the other way round.** Types are inferred; records are structural; keys have one meaning everywhere. You get a compile-time checker without the up-front design and rigidity that often comes with one.
- **Effects you can see, and swap.** Side effects are declared, tracked by the compiler, and substituted with a lexical `withFx` — no mocking frameworks, no plumbing parameters through every function.
- **Code that reads like the spec.** The aim throughout is that domain logic sits front-and-centre, with the incidental machinery — I/O, concurrency plumbing, wiring — factored out to the edges.

There's more on all of this in [the rationale](/about/rationale/).
